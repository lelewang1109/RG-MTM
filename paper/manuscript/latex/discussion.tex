\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Interpreting Reference Alignment}
The certificates isolate displacement forced by subtree contiguity (RQ1), and optimal barriers quantify the scalar price of a chosen order (RQ2). The tested reference-order gains extend across planar and volumetric inputs, while motion and geometry gains depend on the reference and event population (RQ3). Nucleon's unchanged P/R profiles show that useful alignment need not require relaxation.

A band's movement combines projected source movement and changes in its anchor-to-reference residual. Wildfires frames 17--19 show a fixed reference accompanying a shifting P anchor; matched source views separate these effects. For volumetric fields, the reference compresses two further spatial dimensions, so similar projected positions can represent features far apart in the source domain.

Reference alignment complements distance preservation and temporal coherence. On OLR, P/R motion-proxy errors remain 3/56 despite improved order, and ST-MTM retains better SNS, TW, and TD on Storms, OLR, and PM2.5. Annual PM2.5 has 95 clear events at the primary thresholds, with 42 P errors and 47 R20 errors. These outcomes preclude treating a lower NROE as a general guarantee of better geometry, motion interpretation, or reader performance. The motion-margin result in Supplement S1 instead certifies projected distance changes when achieved anchor errors are sufficiently small.

\subsection{Choosing Preservation or Relaxation}
RG-MTM-P is appropriate when source merge thresholds must remain intact. Its position certificate quantifies the displacement that the chosen geometry and hierarchy permit, while its actual anchor errors disclose the result of the budgeted secondary layout. RG-MTM-R adds reference-compatible orders while retaining the extrema and their values. Each relaxed output should be read together with its achieved displacement and mean and maximum merge-level errors; sequence means alone can conceal a large local scalar change.

The eligibility parameter $\kappa$ bounds adjacent parent gaps of nodes that may be flattened. It is neither birth--death persistence nor an output-distortion tolerance. Wider eligibility need not improve the measured trade-off: Cylinder R100 has the same mean NROE as R20 but greater mean NMLE. A hard scalar-error tolerance would require constraining or rejecting candidate orders using their actual optimal filling costs and, subsequently, their rendered errors. Exact frontiers provide such a reference for small trees; the present large-tree construction uses bounded diagnosis and a finite candidate search.

Relaxation changes regular scalar values and connection thresholds. The negative Wildfires connections are artificial filling values, even though the source fire observations are nonnegative. Merge-level error describes these changed pairwise thresholds, while retaining extremum values; it does not establish preservation of scalar distributions, thresholded areas, or every internal feature. An analysis that depends on a physical threshold should consult the source field and the displayed barrier before drawing a conclusion from the relaxed map.

\subsection{Scope of the Evidence}
Spatial projection and preprocessing determine the structures represented by the map. Tangaroa's direction captures 57.686\% of matched motion energy; exact projected anchors still produce 2060/6755 three-dimensional motion-proxy errors. Cylinder's exact $x$ projection likewise retains 603/3196 errors. These controls locate a limit of the chosen reference even when positional layout error vanishes. Multiple references could expose additional spatial relations, but are outside the present evaluation.

The reported populations answer different questions. The 614-frame suite compares four methods on common planar temporal inputs. The 64-frame mirror-motion control isolates absolute anchoring. Tangaroa and Cylinder test independent original-grid volumetric inputs; Nucleon tests one static slice; reconstructed Benzene tests section coherence in a declared classical potential. The 744-hour Storms overview supplies a denser visualization and is excluded from the 118-frame quantitative sequence. These populations are not combined into an overall accuracy score. ST-MTM was not run on the static additions or volumetric sequences.

Data correspondence also limits interpretation. Tangaroa includes 2459/6925 boundary-touching supports and yields 36--186 derived nodes, compared with the reference paper's 50--186. Cylinder uses the 102 public volumes and the declared non-periodic boundary treatment, whereas TMTM reports 508 frames. Benzene reconstructs a related point-charge model rather than the unavailable author potential. The historical January PM2.5 pilot followed a four-month exploration; current primary results instead use every date of 2000 in the same region and fixed processing settings. These declared inputs support the reported comparisons, without establishing exact replication of every original dataset or generalization beyond the tested settings.

Overlap matches provide computational correspondences rather than validated physical tracks. Source-provider event calendars give temporal context rather than cyclone identities, and the Cylinder proxies do not establish vortex labels. Independent physical-track validation and studies of how readers interpret the maps remain necessary extensions. Further persistence settings, internal-feature evaluation, additional scalar models, and preregistered task-specific comparisons would broaden the evidence.

\subsection{Computational Limits and Future Work}
The closed-form fixed-order position cost and optimal non-strict barriers are exact within the stated model; complete small-tree enumeration yields exact frontiers. Large-tree certificate brackets and at most four candidate QPs provide feasible layouts with disclosed unresolved gaps. Higher-degree search and tighter certificates could reduce those gaps. A finer figure preview improves visible detail but does not strengthen the full-profile numerical evidence.

Historical planar QPs can fall back to LP-feasible layouts; complete secondary-optimality records and repeated end-to-end timings would strengthen performance evaluation. The ST-MTM reimplementation uses shared-input parameters and its SciPy OLO heuristic, which exceeds the exhaustive adjacency minimum in eight of 148 audited frames. This audit verifies the declared implementation, without establishing equivalence to every original setting.
