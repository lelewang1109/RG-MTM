Time-varying scalar fields describe pressure, fire activity, radiation, pollution and simulated flow. Static temporal maps compress a spatial domain into one axis, exposing change across time. This compression creates competing requirements: preserving scalar connectivity, retaining spatial relationships, aligning corresponding features, and showing movement along a meaningful spatial reference.

Temporal merge tree maps (TMTM) preserve hierarchical feature grouping and align source-space subtree overlaps across time~\cite{Koepp:2023:TMTM}. Spatiotemporal merge tree maps (ST-MTM) add a distance-aware geometric skeleton and temporal coherence~\cite{STMTM:2026}. These designs establish complementary priorities. Relative distances, however, do not determine a feature's absolute position along a geographic or application-defined axis. When such a reference conflicts with subtree contiguity, even the best topology-preserving layout must displace some features.

We address this design space through a \textbf{unified position--topology framework for Reference-Guided Merge Tree Maps (RG-MTM)}. The same interval variables, four-term quadratic objective and scalar renderer express topology--time alignment, topology--geometry preservation, reference-preserving placement and controlled topology relaxation. Objective weights set preferences; a topology allowance limits labelled merge-level error; a position allowance limits reference displacement. The framework incorporates the design principles of earlier methods as inspired parameter regimes, rather than claiming to reproduce their full algorithms by choosing a threshold.

Our contribution is the connection between these choices and the errors they permit. A positional certificate separates unavoidable packing cost from the extra displacement imposed by hierarchy. Optimal fixed-order barriers quantify the scalar cost of admitting a reference-compatible order. Together they define a feasibility frontier and an inverse query: how much topology allowance is necessary to meet a requested position tolerance? The layout optimizer then selects a geometric and temporal compromise inside the declared budgets.

Three questions organize the evaluation. \textbf{RQ1: Unified operating regimes.} Do parameter restrictions express the intended design priorities, and how similar are the inspired outputs to native baselines? \textbf{RQ2: Budget control.} What position--topology trade-offs are feasible, and do computed witnesses respect their allowances? \textbf{RQ3: Practical consequences.} How do these choices affect reference alignment, geometry and temporal behavior on common inputs and source-linked cases?

The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A unified interval model that expresses topology--time, topology--geometry and reference-guided design principles through objective weights, temporal targets and explicit error budgets, using one solver and renderer.
\item An analysis of position--topology conflict, combining attained positional certificates, optimal fixed-order scalar barriers and forward/inverse feasibility queries with exact or disclosed candidate-set coverage.
\item A controlled evaluation of the model's operating regimes and budget-dependent maps, complemented by independently implemented native baselines, retained temporal comparisons, volumetric and static cases, and source-linked interpretation.
\end{itemize}
The framework describes a parameter space rather than a guaranteed ordering of methods by quality. Its theoretical results concern explicitly defined errors and feasible sets; output similarity and application benefits are empirical questions. Sections~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:framework} define the shared representation and model, Section~\ref{sec:analysis} derives its feasibility tools, and Section~\ref{sec:method} specifies the unified construction and retained eligibility policy.
